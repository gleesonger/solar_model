## Model and data questions requiring decisions

- **Markov DP information and calibration limits:** The [implemented nine-layer DP](../implementation/markov-dynamic-programming.md) averages possible outcomes before command selection and uses separate solar and load transitions with a shipped two-hour reference interval. It assumes future current conditions are observable, including hidden solar layers at night; it is not a full hidden-state information model. Transitions are assumptions rather than fitted probabilities. Recentring forecasts on later real runs is not an explicit simulation of all future forecast updates. Grid interpolation/probability splitting and exact-state stress rollouts remain numerical approximations. Runtime and economic benefit have not been benchmarked. The original [proposal](markov-dp-proposal.md) records the design.

- **Conversion efficiency calibration:** The four shipped values (AC-to-DC 0.970, DC-to-AC 0.974, battery charge 0.970 and battery discharge 0.970) are modelling assumptions, not four independently measured efficiencies for this installation. The DC-to-AC value is a PV-to-AC proxy. The model also treats the solar forecast as energy at the PV DC input and assumes no separate PV-to-bus conversion loss. Compare period energy balances with device telemetry before treating reported stage losses as measured values.

- **Proposed Sigenergy command mapping:** [plan-model section 6.4](../overview/plan-model.md#64-proposed-sigenergy-policy-to-command-mapping) reduces the candidate policies to Self-consumption, Force Charge and Force Discharge; it is not the implemented DP command set. The former Hold-Battery profile is the zero-discharge variant of mode 5, and a zero-charge variant of mode 4 is a proposed grid-supplied hold. Passive Discharge was removed, leaving no policy that expressly limits battery discharge to household shortfalls while curtailing PV charging. The proposed flow priorities are solar to load, then battery, then grid when charging is allowed; household load draws from PV, then battery, then grid when discharging is allowed. Sigenergy's remote-mode protocol does not establish these complete priorities. They differ from the current DP's forced-charge PV allocation and zero-discharge mode-5 PV allocation, so the model must not be claimed as a verified execution of this proposal. Intermediate SOC targets are controller triggers, not writes to the device's cut-off or backup SOC registers. A future controller must monitor SOC and switch commands at a target, including within a planning interval; measurement delay can cause overshoot, and stale telemetry or loss of command ownership needs a defined response. The protocol does not establish the combined zero-ceiling/PV responses. Current DP uses physical 100%/0% stops rather than optimising intermediate SOC targets; its forced rates cap total battery charging/discharging. Verify firmware response and SOC conventions before implementing the proposed policy set.

- **Planning limits (static audit):** Scheduled Min/Max levels are soft preferences. The model enforces the collected grid-point import cap and the configured battery-export cap. An independent site export cap and metadata SOC cut-offs remain outside the physical model. As-of state and dashboard SOC use nameplate capacity, while physical capacity uses state of health. First-step ceilings, PV priorities, zero-power variants and subinterval saturation require installed-firmware verification. See [optimisation](../../solar_model/planning/plan_optimisation_dynamic_programming.py) and [system limits](../../solar_model/planning/system.py).

- **Household register boundaries:** upstream prefers General load 30282; local House Load integrates Total load 30284. Common-load counter 30228 excludes EV charging, without proving loss exclusion. Later diagnostic samples found these alternatives identical to current readings; no source switch or backfill is justified yet. See the [register review](sigenergy-home-assistant-register-review.md).
- **Residual provider-meter differences:** the [2026-11-15 HDF comparison](provider-meter-comparison-2026-11-15.md) finds close overall grid-energy agreement (+0.0461% import, -0.0148% export), with monthly differences changing sign. November 8 export is 0.103 kWh provider versus 0.230 kWh SigenStor despite no collection gaps over 120 seconds. The remaining differences are not explained by this review; no fixed calibration or history rewrite is justified. This grid-only validation does not resolve household-load boundaries or conversion losses.
- **Unavailable electrical inputs:** unused phase readings appear to contain all-one placeholders; two negative PV voltage/current placeholders can multiply into positive power. Confirm field-specific sentinel/domain rules before changing decoding or integration.
- **Diagnostic availability and interpretation:** the [2026-11-15 read-only probe](sigenstor-diagnostics-2026-11-15.md) on firmware `V100R001C00SPC117` returned all ones for export limitation 40038 and illegal data address for merged Alarm7 30281; neither is collected. The all-one export value's meaning remains unverified. Retained status/alarm/temperature fields responded, but a single capture does not verify alarm activation, all state transitions or temperature sentinels. Optional-block failures are isolated in persisted collection; existing required spans still include some diagnostic addresses in gaps, and production retains its existing request timing rather than the probe's 1.05-second spacing. System Info shows latest-known values after failures, without a per-field freshness indication.
- **Transient counter-zero recovery:** `MeasurementStorage.save_modbus_sample` suppresses a decreasing-counter delta but updates its baseline to the lower reading. A temporary 100 → 0 → 100.1 sequence produces a false 100.1 kWh recovery interval. This is a verified code-path risk; occurrence in the supplied history was not established by this review. Define genuine reset versus transient-zero handling before repair.

- **Load export naming:** full-data exports still use the autogenerated label for `plant_load_power_kw`, while chart CSV keeps the internal `load` column. Analysis labels this measured power House Load. Recent Live/Today now hides the separate Load row, resolving its mixed household-power/device-counter-energy presentation; Past summaries retain both Load and House Load energy.
- **Load accounting:** one overnight hour averaged 0.337 kW household power while Load counters advanced 0.70 kWh. AC output minus export agreed with household power; the battery-to-AC residual was about 0.35 kW. Counters may include losses excluded from power; exact firmware accounting remains unverified. Preserve integrated House Load separately from device Load counters and existing counter-based economics. See the [register review](sigenergy-home-assistant-register-review.md).
- **Negative adjusted solar:** [operational_model._prediction_fields](../../solar_model/planning/forecasts/solar.py) does not clamp additive residual output. Negative W/Wh is possible by inspection; decide physical bounds and test adverse residuals.
- **Replay parity:** live correction handles future target days in each collection; historical rebuild clears all selected targets but replays only each collection's current local day. Confirm the intended audit coverage before relying on rebuilt future-day vintages.
- **DST representation:** several views use fixed 24 clock-hour buckets, merging repeated hours. Spring DST SQL tests exist; UTC planning arithmetic and both DST transitions now have regression coverage ; fixed-clock dashboard views and broader local display still need separate verification.
- **Interval attribution:** counter gaps use the ending timestamp's tariff/hour and PV integration spans the whole gap. Decide acceptable maximum gap and whether tariff/day boundaries require interval splitting.
- **Availability downloads:** state availability uses the last reading; changing to a mean requires an explicit contract decision. Earlier averaging expectations were not proven numerical defects.
- **Forecast export meaning:** export aggregation averages cumulative `*_watt_hours` while charts difference them into interval energy. Confirm whether downloads should expose stored provider fields or reproduce chart energy.
- **Coarse planning lookahead:** the hidden 24-hour extension uses hourly steps in the shipped configuration with interval-start tariffs/limits and aggregated solar/load. It deliberately does not split at tariff boundaries; within-slot cheap charging, demand/solar ordering and changes in limits are approximated. The hidden solar day now repeats today's actual-plus-adjusted hourly profile; this assumes similar solar conditions, and sample means approximate energy when collection is irregular. Missing actual hours fall back to the selected forecast, whose uncovered edges still pad to zero. The hidden horizon and terminal credit reduce depletion incentives but do not remove these coarse-slot approximations. See [plan model](../overview/plan-model.md#44-daily-load-forecast).
- **DP approximation:** The solver uses discrete forced rates and interpolates continuation values, so the result is optimal only within the numerical model’s approximations. See the [current DP contract](../implementation/dynamic-programming.md).
- **Plan model implementation decisions:** the [current planning reference](../overview/plan-model.md#6-validation-and-open-decisions) documents the implemented shared next action, production forecast reuse, separate solar and load multipliers and tariff-scaled score reporting. Planning remains report-only. Remaining decisions concern physical command response/handover, consistent SOC conventions, input policies and numerical tolerances.
- **Validation policy:** unknown YAML keys are ignored and some numeric/site fields lack bounds. Decide whether descriptive keys should be formal fields and other unknown keys rejected.

## Coverage and maintenance gaps

- **Idle CPU attribution:** The [staged Windows investigation](dashboard-staged-idle-cpu.md) found sleeping workers/collectors and stopped browser-driven work, but dashboard idle samples varied substantially. A clean final pair measured 0.21% of one core for Hello World and 0.91% for the dashboard. Routine callbacks and database queries were identified, but their contribution does not explain the entire higher minute; Linux/container CPU and native/thread-level attribution remain unverified. Invalid profiler timings and process-isolation qualifications are retained in the report.

- Chrome Android saved offline pages can preserve dashboard visuals while in-page scripts are inactive. Finite CSS activity indication and an absolute Last reading timestamp reduce ambiguity, but actual saved-page/freeze behaviour still needs verification on Android hardware; desktop Chromium regression checks do not establish it.

- **Android installed-app shell:** the dashboard declares an explicit standalone manifest and transparent install icons. A read-only HTTPS check found Cloudflare Access protecting the deployed manifest/icons. The omitted manifest credentials attribute was reproduced as a manifest parse failure behind synthetic cookie authentication and corrected with `crossorigin="use-credentials"`; full local Chrome/Chromium now install and launch both public and cookie-protected fixtures. Android toolbar removal/reinstallation, the WebAPK service and authenticated manifest delivery through the actual HTTPS proxy still need device/deployment verification. The historical change that introduced the original screenshot's title/address toolbar was not established.
- **Recent browser fixture precision:** `tests/browser/test_daily_load_forecast_browser.py` expects `0.3` for a live House Load input of `0.35`, while current rendering shows `0.35`. This existing assertion stops the broader responsive/legend check before it completes; update the fixture to the current precision contract separately.

- Runtime dependencies remain ranged, with no lockfile. HiGHS is now explicit; package extras declare test, browser and research dependencies.
- Tariff helper logic is duplicated between config validation and economics; changes must preserve matching parity.
- `solar_model/storage/queries/dashboard.py` retains older ORM/pandas calculations used by current adapters or SQL regression comparisons. Remove individual paths only after checking both consumers and independent test references; the unmounted Hourly tab has been removed.
- Independent `check-tariffs` scripts run on import and extraction has a hard-coded external database path; treat them as manual tools.

## Suggested repair order

1. Validate the declared mode and ceiling response against installed firmware, including zero-power variants and PV flow priority.
2. Resolve forecast interval meaning; extend replay parity and adverse optimiser coverage before changing those contracts.
3. Review dependency reproducibility and retained legacy paths; HiGHS declaration and Docker port metadata have already been corrected.


## First-step action modelling

The system-port model reconstructs report routes after solving. All scenarios share one first action, with scenario-specific physical saturation. The returned SOC target is for a future controller to monitor; it does not constrain the present interval or write an inverter cut-off. The solver scores breaches of scheduled minimum and maximum energy levels. Restrictive battery-export limits constrain shared discharge rates and resulting flows. See [planning implementation](../implementation/planning.md#numerical-and-report-contracts).

**Remaining verification:** the ceiling interpretation, precise firmware priorities, zero-power variants and response when a future controller switches at its SOC target require installed-device validation. Ramping, delays, transient AC limits and subinterval clipping are not simulated. Metadata SOC conventions, input-age policy and site-export settings remain open decisions.

Planning remains report-only: no device commands, acknowledgement, EMS ownership or handover are implemented. Real device/provider behaviour, Docker installation, actual process-kill recovery, sustained concurrent SQLite writes and exhaustive browser/output combinations remain unverified. Dashboard administrative confirmation is not authentication; intended access policy remains undecided.

## Scope and exclusions

Completed counterexample solves returned optimal status and each finished within the repository's five-second simulation limit. These targeted checks do not establish annual savings or installed-firmware response. Grid import/export regression coverage is maintained in `tests/planning/test_grid_exchange.py`; see the implementation guide for its current direction/billing contract.

Fixed scenario weights, two-stage recourse, reserve utility, terminal valuation and coarse hidden slots remain documented design choices. This review does not establish that replacing them improves decisions. SOC/nameplate versus health-adjusted capacity remains inconsistent, but the inspected installation metadata reports 100% health, so it was not promoted as a material current-installation finding. Forecast timestamp disagreement, site-limit settings and stale-input policy require separate evidence before claiming a quantified economic or system-capability failure.

- **Zero-power command hardware verification:** Mode 4 at zero charge holds both battery directions in the model; mode 5 at zero discharge allows surplus PV charging while preventing discharge. V2.9 documents the modes and ceilings, but installed-firmware flow behaviour and restoration between profiles remain unverified. The application sends no battery-control commands.

- **Mode 5 surplus-PV response assumption:** The model assumes surplus PV charges the battery before export at zero discharge. The protocol does not establish this full flow order. Confirm it on installed firmware before relying on this profile.
